# Market, pain points and content flow

Researched 29 Oct 2026. Goal of the site: **lead generation**, getting Canadian job seekers to submit their details.

## 1. Does a service like ours already exist in Canada?

Yes. The space is crowded, so "remote part-time work" alone is not unique. The gap is in the experience, not the product.

| Type | Examples | How it works for the job seeker | Where it falls short for a second-job seeker |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public job boards | [Job Bank](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/job-bank.html), Indeed Canada, Workopolis (now run by Indeed) | Free. You search, filter and apply to each posting yourself. | Many results are not truly remote or part-time. You apply many times and often hear nothing. |
| Vetted remote boards | [FlexJobs](https://www.flexjobs.com/remote-jobs/world/canada) | Paid subscription. Listings are hand-screened for scams. | You pay before you find anything. Many jobs are also listed free elsewhere. Some "remote" US roles exclude Canada. |
| Freelance marketplaces | Upwork | Project-based work. | You must pitch, build a profile and compete on price. It is not steady hours beside a job. |
| Staffing agencies | [Randstad Canada](https://www.randstad.ca/randstad-app/find-jobs/), [Adecco Canada](https://www.adecco.com/en-ca/job-seekers/how-to-apply), [Express Employment](https://www.expresspros.ca/job-seekers/faqs) | No fee to the job seeker (employers pay). Randstad's app books shifts and pays weekly. A recruiter matches you. | Mostly on-site, light industrial and temp work. Not built around remote second jobs. |
| Gig and platform work | Delivery, rideshare, marketplace selling | Start fast, flexible. | You are self-employed and handle your own tax and platform reporting. |

What these already promise and we cannot claim as unique: "no fee", "flexible", "paid weekly", "choose your hours".

## 2. What job seekers are frustrated about (use these)

| Pain point | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Ghost jobs | 56% of Canadian workers suspect they've applied for a role that was never real (Employment Hero, Angus Reid Forum, Jan 2026, n=1,004). |
| Silence | 70% say being ghosted hurts their mental health and motivation. 78% say hiring friction has put them off looking before. (Same survey) |
| Hidden pay | 64% have skipped a job because the posting showed no salary. (Same survey) |
| Faceless process | 35% say automated hiring leaves them feeling unheard. 32% think companies only collect résumés. (Express-Harris Poll, May to June 2026, n=508) |
| Wrong fit | 33% say finding a job that matches their skills is their biggest challenge. 22% cite being ghosted. (Robert Half, March 2025, n=835) |
| Scams | Job fraud was the 4th costliest fraud type in Canada in 2025: 2,148 reports, 1,726 victims, $50.6 million reported lost. Losses were $7.3 million in 2022. (Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre) |

Law catching up: Ontario now requires employers with 25 or more employees to show pay ranges in postings and respond to interviewed candidates within 45 days (as described in the Employment Hero release). Transparency is becoming the baseline, not a bonus.

## 3. What a job seeker expects before they hand over details

This is the checklist the page must answer. Items in the last column need your confirmation before we write them.

| Expectation | Why it matters | Can we promise it? |
|---|---|---|
| Is this real, or a scam? | Scam fear is high and rising. | Confirm: company name, official domain, contact details to show. |
| Will I be asked to pay or deposit? | Deposits and "unlock fees" are the scam signature (CAFC). | Confirm: "never" is the only safe answer. |
| What is the pay, in CAD? | 64% skip postings without pay. | Confirm: a real rate or range. |
| How often am I paid and by whom? | Weekly pay is a staffing-agency standard now. | Confirm. |
| How many hours, and can I choose them? | They already have a job. | Confirm: minimum and maximum hours. |
| Will you actually reply? | Ghosting is the top emotional complaint. | Confirm a reply time you can keep, even for a "no". |
| Can I keep my day job? | Conflicts with an employer are a real worry. | Confirm. |
| Is it open in my province? | Many "remote" listings quietly exclude Canada. | Confirm: provinces covered. |
| Who is my employer? | Employee versus contractor changes tax and benefits. | Confirm. |
| What happens to my details? | Privacy. | Confirm: who sees them, retention, consent wording. |

## 4. The big idea: "the second job that doesn't ghost you"

Every competitor sells jobs. Nobody leads with the thing job seekers are angriest about: silence, hidden pay and fear of scams. We win trust by showing the whole deal before asking for anything, then keeping our word after they submit.

Positioning line options:
- "The second job that doesn't ghost you."
- "See the pay, the hours and the catch before you fill in a single field."
- "Day job pays the bills. Second job pays you. And we write back."

## 5. Creative features that show uniqueness (pick two or three)

1. **Goal-first calculator ("Weeks to my goal").** Replace the generic estimator with a goal picker: first-home fund, debt-free date, cottage weekend, trip home, own business. The visitor enters a goal amount and weekly hours, and the page shows how many weeks it could take, with the same rate × hours maths. This ties the earlier "real reason" copy to a tool, and the goal chosen becomes a lead field that personalizes follow-up. Low build effort: reuses the existing calculator script.
2. **A real role card, shown before the form.** One plain card with pay in CAD, weekly hours, remote or not, who employs you, pay frequency, start timing, and "what you pay: $0". It answers ghost jobs and hidden pay head on. Needs your real facts.
3. **The "We will never" list.** Five lines taken straight from the Anti-Fraud Centre warning signs: never ask for a deposit, a crypto transfer, software to install, recruiting others, or to forward money through your bank account. Add how to verify us (official domain, phone). This turns scam fear into a reason to choose us.
4. **A reply promise with a visible tracker.** After submitting: Received, Reviewed, Reply by [date]. Even a "no" gets an answer. The thank-you page shows the same three steps. Only use a time you can keep.
5. **"Built around Canadian life" hours picker.** Options like shift worker, after the kids are in bed, weekends, winter slow season. Already half-built on the Your hours page.
6. **Early-answer FAQ in plain talk.** "What's the catch?" with an honest answer builds more trust than a clean sales line.

## 6. Recommended content flow (one page, with the form as the finish)

1. **Hook, pain first.** "Day job pays the bills. Second job pays you." Subline: "And we write back." One stat line: "56% of Canadians think they've applied for a ghost job."
2. **Goal calculator.** Pick the goal, set hours, see the weeks. A button to continue.
3. **The real deal card.** Pay, hours, who employs you, paid when, $0 to join.
4. **Why choose us (three proofs, not adjectives).** We show pay up front. We never ask for money. We reply, even with a no.
5. **We will never.** The five-line scam-proof list and how to verify us.
6. **Who this fits.** Short lines for shift workers, parents, students, people paying off debt, side-business starters.
7. **How it works.** Form, review, reply by a date, start. Tied to the tracker.
8. **Honest FAQ.** What's the catch, can I keep my job, do I report the income (yes), who pays me.
9. **Form.** Short: first name, email, province, hours, goal. Consent wording near the button.

The current multi-page layout can stay. This flow can run as one long landing page for ads, with the other pages kept as supporting pages.

## 7. Warnings before launch

- **Fictional reviews.** The reviews page uses invented "sample" reviews and chats. Ad platforms and Canadian advertising rules treat fake testimonials as misleading, even when labelled. Replace with real, permitted feedback or remove before any ad runs.
- **Earnings.** Keep the illustration labelled and tied to a real rate. Never state a typical result you cannot show.
- **Uniqueness has to be true.** Every promise above (reply time, pay shown up front, no fees) must match what the business actually does. A promise we break makes us the thing we're criticizing.

## 8. Questions only you can answer

1. Who employs the person, and is it employee or contractor work?
2. What kind of work is it, and what is the real pay rate or range in CAD?
3. How often are they paid?
4. How many hours a week, minimum and maximum?
5. Which provinces can you cover?
6. What reply time can you honestly keep, and does everyone get a reply?
7. Are there any real testimonials we can use?
8. Do you have an official company name and domain to show?
