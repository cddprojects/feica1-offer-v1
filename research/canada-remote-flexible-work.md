# Canada: remote, online and flexible work. What is actually new, and what is not

Researched 29 Oct 2026. Every figure below links to a government or Statistics Canada source. Use these as the only factual claims on the site.

## Short answer on "bonuses, rewards and new updates"

There is **no federal or provincial bonus, reward or payment for doing remote, online or flexible work**. Any signing bonus, referral reward or incentive comes from an individual employer and is not guaranteed. The site must not imply otherwise. What exists are rights, tax rules and wage floors, listed below.

## Rights and rules that are real updates

1. **Right to request flexible work (federal only).** Employees of federally regulated employers can ask in writing for changes to hours, schedule or work location after six months of continuous employment. The employer must answer in writing within 30 days and cannot punish the request. It does not apply to most private-sector jobs, which fall under provincial law. It is a right to ask, not a right to remote work. Sources: [Canada Labour Code s.177.1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/L-2/section-177.1.html), [Canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/corporate/portfolio/labour/programs/labour-standards/flexible-work-arrangements.html).
2. **No general right to work from home.** Employers are free to offer or withdraw remote arrangements. Source: [Chambers Employment 2026, Canada](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/employment-2026/canada/trends-and-developments).
3. **Fully remote staff and equal treatment (2026 regulations).** New rules say how a fully remote federal employee is assigned to a worksite for equal-treatment and temporary-help-agency rules. Source: [Canada Gazette Part II, 6 May 2026](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2026/2026-05-06/html/sor-dors75-eng.html).

## Money and tax rules a second-income reader should know

1. **Home office deduction for employees.** The $2-a-day flat rate ended after 2022. For 2023 onward, employees use the detailed method. They need a signed Form T2200 and must work from home more than 50% of the time for at least four consecutive weeks, and the employer must have required it. Source: [CRA, home office expenses](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/deductions-credits-expenses/line-22900-other-employment-expenses/work-space-home-expenses.html).
2. **Side and gig income is taxable.** If you are self-employed through a platform or contract, report all of it on Form T2125 and your return, including tips and gifts. Source: [CRA, gig economy](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/platform-economy/gig-economy.html).
3. **GST/HST small supplier limit.** You do not need to register if taxable revenue stays at or below $30,000 over four consecutive quarters. Source: [CRA, when to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html).
4. **Platforms report sellers to the CRA.** Under the Reporting Rules for Digital Platform Operators, platforms send seller income details to the CRA and give you a copy by 31 January. Source: [CRA tax tip](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2024/using-app-website-earn-income.html).
5. **Reporting income protects benefits.** The CRA says accurate income reporting is how you receive credits and benefits you qualify for, such as the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit. Source: [CRA, self-employed tips 2026](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/self-employed-make-filing-easier-with-these-tips-from-the-cra.html).

## Copy approach (the "life lesson" angle)

Rule: say one true, plain thing a person would say over coffee, then tie it to a job. No promises.

- One income is one point of failure. Most people already know this and few act on it.
- You do not need a second career. A few steady hours beat one big plan you never start.
- Pay yourself first. Part of anything extra goes to debt or savings before it disappears.
- Be honest about hours. A second income only lasts if it fits the life you already have.
- Check before you commit. Ask for duties, hours and pay in writing.

Claims to avoid: guaranteed income, "quit your job", "easy money", named earnings as typical, any bonus or reward not tied to a named employer.
